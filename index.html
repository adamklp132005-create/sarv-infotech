Who Was Mahatma Gandhi?

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, known to the world as Mahatma Gandhi, was born on October 2, 1869, in Porbandar, Gujarat, India. He led India's struggle for independence from British rule through peaceful and non-violent resistance, inspiring civil rights and freedom movements across the world.

His philosophy of Satyagraha (truth-force) and non-violence changed the course of history. He is fondly remembered as the "Father of the Nation" in India, and his birthday, October 2nd, is celebrated as Gandhi Jayanti, a national holiday.

Why I Admire Him

  • He proved that peaceful protest could defeat an empire without a single weapon.
  • He lived a life of simplicity, honesty, and self-discipline, practicing what he preached.
  • He fought against untouchability and worked tirelessly for social equality.
  • He inspired global leaders like Martin Luther King Jr. and Nelson Mandela.
  • He taught the world that courage and compassion can go hand in hand.

The Dandi March (Salt March), 1930

One of Gandhi's most iconic acts of civil disobedience was the Dandi March. From March 12 to April 6, 1930, he walked 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to the coastal village of Dandi with 78 followers to protest the British salt tax by making salt from seawater. This simple act of defiance sparked a nationwide movement against colonial rule.

Life Timeline

  • 1869 — Born in Porbandar, Gujarat, India.
  • 1893 — Moved to South Africa; began fighting racial discrimination.
  • 1915 — Returned to India and joined the freedom movement.
  • 1930 — Led the historic Dandi (Salt) March.
  • 1942 — Launched the "Quit India" Movement.
  • 1947 — India gained independence from British rule.
  • 1948 — Assassinated on January 30 in New Delhi.
